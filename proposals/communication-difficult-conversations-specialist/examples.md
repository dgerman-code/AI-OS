# Examples

Status: `PROPOSED`
Version: 0.1

> **Original illustrative material.** These examples were written for this package to demonstrate
> the methodology's rules. They reproduce no book, course, transcript, proprietary framework or
> protected phrasing, and they are not attributed to any author. They are **representative, not
> exhaustive, and not a library to copy from**: every real message is drafted against its own
> record, its own constraints and its own supplied conclusions.
>
> No example here is approved wording, and none may be sent without the review path of
> `role-card.md` RC-5 and the gate resolved by `decision-right-gap-analysis.md` §4 — which, for
> anything released outside the entity under its name, is `decision.external_publication`.

## 1. How to read these

Each example shows a **situation**, the **move** the methodology selects, and the **principle or
technique** it comes from. Where an example shows improved wording, the improvement is the
*structure*, not the sentence: the same structure produces different words on a different record.

**Rule EX-1 — no example asserts a fact.** Every date, name and figure below is a placeholder of
the form `[X]`. An example that carried a concrete fact would invite it being reused where it is
false.

## 2. Situation patterns and the move selected

| # | Situation | Move | Basis |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hostile accusation with one material point buried in it | Correct only the material fact; redirect to the decision required | P-3, T-7, T-8 |
| 2 | Repeated "I don't understand" on a point answered and dated | State the point once; ask the remaining binary question | T-10, `POSSIBLE_STRATEGIC_CONFUSION` |
| 3 | Partner proposal that must be declined | Acknowledge, clear no, optional narrower alternative | T-13, T-23 |
| 4 | Board disagreement | Isolate the disputed decision; remove personal attribution | T-20, P-14 |

| 6 | Information not received | State what is outstanding, for what, by when | T-14 |
| 7 | Terms changed between drafts | State the difference between versions, not the motive | T-6, T-11 |

| 9 | A closed point reopened | Name the closure date; restate the remaining issue | T-9, `REOPENED_CLOSED_ISSUE` |

## 3. Structural rewrites

The left column is the reactive draft a user typically produces. The right column is what the
methodology does to it — and the **reason** is the point, not the wording.

| Reactive | Controlled | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "You ignored us again." | "We have not received a response to the requests of `[date]` and `[date]`." | Accusation → fact. T-5, P-7 |
| "You keep changing the deal." | "The current draft introduces terms not present in the version of `[date]`." | Motive → version comparison. T-11 |
| "This behaviour is unacceptable." | "We are not able to continue under the current process." | Judgement of a person → statement of self-action. P-4, P-14 |
| "We are extremely disappointed and shocked." | "The current outcome does not match the agreed process." | Emotional register → operational statement. P-2 |
| "As I have already explained several times…" | "To avoid ambiguity, the position is `[X]`." | Defensive history → single clear restatement. P-8 |
| "You must answer by Friday." | "We need written confirmation by `[date]` to keep `[next step]` on schedule." | Command → requirement with a stated reason. T-14 |
| "Obviously you misunderstood." | "Our position is `[X]`. The remaining question is `[Y]`." | Attribution of error → restatement plus the open question. T-8 |
| "Everyone agrees this is your fault." | "The record shows the following sequence: `[dated entries]`." | Consensus claim → verifiable chronology. P-7 |
| A five-paragraph refusal | Clear no, one rationale, optional alternative | Attack surface reduced. P-8, T-13 |
| A point-by-point rebuttal of insults | Material facts corrected; the rest left unanswered | Agenda retained. P-3 |

**Rule EX-2 — the right column is not softer.** In several rows it is firmer. The methodology
removes heat, not force.

## 4. Boundary formulations

Self-directed, and each one holds without the other party agreeing to it (P-4):

1. "We will not proceed without written confirmation of `[X]`."
2. "I am not able to continue this discussion in this format; please send the points in writing."
3. "We can discuss the proposal, and we will not commit before `[review]` is complete."
4. "We will respond to project issues; personal remarks will not be addressed."
5. "We will not reopen the agreed scope without a formal change request."
6. "We can meet this week, and we cannot accept a same-day decision deadline."
7. "We will provide the requested data once the disclosure basis is confirmed."
8. "We can continue negotiations, and not on the basis that `[disputed term]` is already agreed."
9. "We will use `[designated channel]` for this matter going forward."
10. "If `[confirmation]` is not received by `[date]`, we will pause `[next step]`."

**Rule EX-3 — note what is absent.** None of these tells the other party what to do, and none
states a consequence the user cannot bring about (S-3). Item 10 states one the user can: pausing
their own next step. Items 7 and 3 defer to someone else's conclusion rather than asserting one.

## 5. Negotiation patterns

| Situation | Move |
|---|---|
| Anchor pressure | Acknowledge the proposal; state non-acceptance; counter on structure rather than on the number |
| Artificial urgency | Ask whether the deadline is fixed, and by what decision path |
| Repeated demands for concessions | Summarise what has already moved and what is now fixed |
| Topic switching | Park the new issue; close the current term first (T-9, T-20) |
| "Take it or leave it" | State whether the terms meet the stated minimum conditions; make no counter-ultimatum |
| Vague promise | Request a written, measurable commitment (T-17) |
| Conditional threat | Separate the stated consequence from the actual decision authority behind it |
| Personal attack | Do not defend the personality; return to the term under negotiation (T-8) |
| Endless clarification | State one formulation; request explicit acceptance or rejection (T-10) |
| Relationship leverage | Recognise the relationship's value without trading a substantive safeguard |

## 6. Two worked cases

### 6.1 A hostile message containing a material fact

**Situation.** A counterparty sends a long accusatory email. Buried in paragraph four is: *"your
own submission of `[date]` confirmed the revised scope."* If that is true, the user's position on
scope is different from what they believe it to be.

**What the methodology does, in order.**

1. `POSSIBLE_DEFLECTION` is *not* the first move. The record is read for facts first (RC-3, S-7).
2. The claim is checked against the record. If a submission of that date exists and says that, it
   is recorded as an `EVIDENCE` item bound to that paragraph plus a **new linked** `FACT_CLAIM`,
   and it enters `position_changing_claims` (DC-4, DC-5). The counterparty's email stays `SOURCE`;
   nothing is relabelled.
3. The diagnosis is escalated to the substantive owning Role **before any drafting**, and
   `recommended_response` stays `null` (DC-5).
4. Only once the owning Role has re-derived the position does drafting resume — on the new
   position, not on the old one.

**Why this is the case worth writing down.** A calm, well-structured, beautifully controlled reply
asserting a position the record has already falsified is worse than an angry one, because it is
more credible and it is on the record.

### 6.2 De-escalation that would have cost a protection

**Situation.** A draft response to a partner contains: *"We reserve all rights in respect of
`[matter]`."* The user asks for the message to be "warmer, we still have to work with them."

**What the methodology does.**

1. The reservation is a material protection (P-15, MC-3).
2. Warmth is applied everywhere it does not touch the reservation: opening, acknowledgement of the
   working relationship, the forward-looking close.
3. The reservation sentence is **unchanged**.
4. The Communication Control Filter records ESCALATION lower than it would otherwise be, with a
   `protection_conflicts` entry naming the revision that was **not** made and why
   (`communication-control-filter.md` §7).
5. The user is told, in one line, that the sentence was left alone and why.

**Why this is the case worth writing down.** A filter that optimises comfort will eventually
optimise away the protection. The recorded conflict is what makes that visible to a reviewer.

## 7. What these examples deliberately do not include

- any wording attributed to a named individual;
- any protected or proprietary phrasing;
- any concrete fact, date, figure or party name;
- any example of a psychological characterisation, even as a "wrong answer";
- any example of a message being sent, since no example in this package can be sent.
