# Exemplar 7 — Retracted canonical statement preserving history

Status: PROPOSED — Phase 8 exemplar knowledge record
Inherits: `standard.knowledge.common_constraints@0.1`

**Proves:** that retraction differs from supersession, that it leaves a governed gap, and that it erases nothing.

## Identity
- Canonical ID: `knowledge.eligibility_basis.programme_call_x`
- Version: 1 — `RETRACTED`
- Subject: the basis on which the entity is eligible under a named funding call
- Scope: `PROGRAMME/<x>`
- Sensitivity: `CONFIDENTIAL`

## What happened
Version 1 was promoted on a reading of the call rulebook. A later rulebook clarification showed the reading was wrong. **No corrected version could be promoted**, because on the corrected reading the entity's eligibility basis is not established at all — there is nothing to state.

So the status was withdrawn. This is the **second** canonical authority (`knowledge/canonical-promotion-governance.md` §3): withdrawal into a gap, with no successor. Every other canonical act has a successor and is therefore a promotion.

## The gap, stated
The programme scope now has **no canonical position** on eligibility basis. The retraction record says so explicitly, and names what depended on it: a submitted application, a partner confirmation, and a workstream whose plan assumed eligibility.

**The unnoticed gap is the specific harm this authority carries.** A retraction that does not name what it leaves behind has removed a position and left the dependencies pointing at nothing — which reads, downstream, exactly like a subject nobody ever addressed.

## What retraction did not do
- **It did not delete anything.** Version 1 stays visible, with its content, its evidence and its reason for withdrawal.
- **It did not make the opposite true.** Withdrawing a claim of eligibility is not a finding of ineligibility; the question is now open, and that is a different and worse position than either answer.
- **It did not rewrite the past.** The application was submitted in reliance on a then-canonical statement. That is what happened, and the record continues to show it — the retraction is dated, and everything before that date was done under the earlier position.
- **It did not resolve anything.** The eligibility question returns to the Role that owns it, with the clarification as new evidence.

## Why not supersession
Supersession requires a successor. Calling this a supersession would imply the scope still has a position, which is the misrepresentation the distinction exists to prevent.
